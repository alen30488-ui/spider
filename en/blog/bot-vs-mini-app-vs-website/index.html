Bot, Mini App or website — which one does your business need?

We compare a Telegram bot, a Telegram Mini App and a website by use case, price, timeline and discoverability. When a bot is enough, when you need a Mini App and when a website is a must — with a table, proven combinations and a checklist.

Many conversations with our clients start with the same question: “Do we need a bot or a website?” The short answer is that it depends on where your customers are and what they need to do. Below we compare the three formats by use case, budget and timeline, show the combinations that work best and give you a checklist to decide in five minutes.

How a bot, a Mini App and a website differ

A Telegram bot is a conversation in a chat: messages, buttons, commands and notifications. A bot asks questions, takes requests, answers common questions and can message a customer first — once the customer has started the bot.

A Telegram Mini App is a web app that opens full-screen inside Telegram. It has its own interface: a catalog with filters, a cart, a booking calendar, a customer account. There is nothing to install and no sign-up — Telegram passes the user’s data to the app.

A website is your own place on the internet, independent of any messenger. People find it through Google and other search engines, ads lead to it, it is easy to share, and many customers check a company’s website before they buy.

One detail about payments: digital goods and services inside Telegram are sold for Telegram Stars, while physical goods can be paid for through connected payment providers. A website has no such restrictions — any payment gateway will do.

When a Telegram bot is enough

A bot is the fastest and most affordable way to automate communication if your customers already message you on Telegram. It handles tasks where the user answers a few questions or taps a couple of buttons:

  • take a request and pass it to a manager or your CRM;
  • book a customer in and remind them about the appointment;
  • answer common questions around the clock and hand over to a human when needed;
  • send order updates, reminders and newsletters to subscribers;
  • automate your team’s routine: staff requests, reports, approvals.

The bot’s limit is the interface. A long catalog, filters, seat selection or comparing plans in a chat is inconvenient: users have to scroll through dozens of messages. That is when a Mini App is worth considering.

When you need a Mini App

A Mini App is essentially a website inside Telegram. It costs more than a bot but gives you a full interface and opens in one tap from a bot, a channel, a chat or a link. Businesses choose a Mini App when:

  • customers come from a Telegram channel, and you need to sell from a catalog with filters, a cart and checkout;
  • booking requires picking a specialist, a service, a date and a time slot;
  • you need a customer account, bonuses or a loyalty program without asking customers to install a separate app;
  • the product is a course, a game or an interactive service.

That is how the FixPoint project works, for example: the bot takes requests and sends repair status updates, while booking a technician happens in a Mini App. Keep security in mind: the server must verify the signature of the data the Mini App receives from Telegram, otherwise users can be impersonated. We cover this in “7 common Telegram bot vulnerabilities”.

When a website is a must

Bots and Mini Apps live inside Telegram and barely show up in search results. You need a website if:

  • customers look for you in search engines — for queries like “iPhone repair near me” or “website development for a clinic”;
  • you plan to run search ads: an ad needs a clear landing page;
  • trust matters: prices, case studies, reviews and company details — many people check the website before they get in touch;
  • part of your audience does not use Telegram, and deals go through e-mail, tenders and proposals.

Combinations that work best

More often than not, the winner is a combination of two formats:

  • Website + bot. The website brings customers from search and ads, the bot takes requests, sends booking reminders and brings customers back with newsletters.
  • Bot + Mini App. The bot is the entry point and the notification channel, the Mini App is the catalog, cart or booking with a convenient interface. Most stores in Telegram work this way.
  • Website + Mini App on a shared backend. The catalog, prices and orders are shared, and customers choose the channel they like. A second interface on top of an existing backend costs much less than a separate project.

Checklist: choose in five minutes

  1. Where are your customers now? If they are in a Telegram channel or chat, start with a bot or a Mini App. If they search online, you need a website.
  2. What should the customer do? Leave a request or ask a question — a bot is enough. Choose from a catalog, pay or book a specific time — a Mini App or a website.
  3. Do you need traffic from search and ads? If so, a website is a must; a bot can be added to it.
  4. What is your starting budget? Under ₽50,000 — a bot or a landing page. From ₽120,000 — a Mini App or a multi-page website.
  5. How will you bring customers back? A bot can message customers first — to remind them of an appointment or offer a repeat purchase. A website needs e-mail campaigns or ads for that.
