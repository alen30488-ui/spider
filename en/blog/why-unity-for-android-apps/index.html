Why we build Android apps with Unity

Why we develop Android apps with Unity: smooth animations, 3D and game mechanics, a consistent look on all devices and ready-made SDKs for Firebase and RuStore. An honest look at the limitations of this approach and how we deal with them.

Unity is best known as a game engine, so people often ask us: why use it to build apps for bookings, orders and loyalty programs? The short answer: because users choose apps that are enjoyable to use. Here is what Unity gives a business, what the limitations of this approach are and when we advise choosing a different format.

What Unity is

Unity is an engine and development environment used to create mobile games, interactive apps, AR projects and simulators. The code is written in C#, and the project is built into an Android app. It is one of the most popular engines in mobile development: it has a large community, detailed documentation and ready-made solutions for almost any task.

We use Unity not only for games. It also powers quite “businesslike” apps: for example, the app for the FORMA fitness studio chain with a schedule, a waiting list and a membership with a QR code, and the app for ZERNO coffee shops with pre-ordering and a loyalty card.

What it gives a business

  • Smoothness and animations. Unity renders the interface itself, so transitions, animations and effects are not limited to a set of standard elements, and the app runs at 60 frames per second.
  • 3D and augmented reality. 3D product models you can rotate with a finger, interactive diagrams and floor plans, AR try-on — via AR Foundation and ARCore.
  • Game mechanics. Progress, levels, rewards and mini-games in a loyalty program give users a reason to come back. For a game engine, this is a natural task rather than an expensive add-on.
  • A consistent look on all devices. The interface looks and behaves the same on different Android versions and in the custom skins of different manufacturers.
  • Ready-made SDKs. Push notifications, analytics and crash reports via Firebase; push notifications, payments and reviews via RuStore — these services have versions for Unity.
  • Content without app updates. New banners, promotions and 3D scenes can be loaded from the server without releasing a new version in the stores.

Limitations — and how we deal with them

Every approach has a price. Here is what you have to take into account with Unity and what we do about it.

LimitationWhat the issue is and how we solve it
App sizeThe build includes the engine, so it weighs more. We compress textures, load heavy content on demand and build an AAB so that the store delivers only the files a device needs
BatteryBy default, the engine redraws the screen constantly. On static screens, we lower the frame rate and redraw only when something changes
Startup timeThe engine needs time to start. We make a lightweight splash screen and load data and heavy scenes in the background
Text inputInput fields need attention. We use the native Android keyboard and test input on different devices
Low-end phonesGraphics can strain them. We test on budget devices, not just flagships, and adjust graphics quality to the phone's performance

When Unity is the best choice

  • animations, 3D, AR or game mechanics matter in the app;
  • you need a memorable branded interface rather than standard screens;
  • a loyalty program with gamification: levels, rewards, challenges;
  • educational, entertainment and children's apps;
  • presenting products, real estate or equipment in 3D.

When to choose a different format

We tell you honestly when you do not need a mobile app:

  • Your audience is already on Telegram, and the task is booking or a catalog. It is faster and cheaper to build a Mini App: it opens without installation. More on this in the article “What is a Telegram Mini App”.
  • Customers look for you on Google and Yandex. You need a website — apps are not found through search.
  • The idea needs to be tested in a couple of weeks. Start with an MVP in Telegram and build the app once demand is confirmed. How to choose features for the first version is covered in the article “Mobile app MVP”.

Our stack

Unity and C#; URP for graphics, UI Toolkit for the interface, Addressables for content loading, DOTween for animations, Firebase and RuStore SDKs for notifications, analytics and payments. We write the backend in Go with a PostgreSQL database.
