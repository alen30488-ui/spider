How a security audit works: stages, timelines and the report

What happens during a security audit of a website, Telegram bot or Mini App: agreeing on scope and access, reconnaissance, automated scanning, manual logic testing, a report with CVSS risk levels and a re-check after fixes.

The word “audit” makes many people uneasy: it seems that the review will bring the website down or end with a pile of incomprehensible reports. In fact, an audit is a clear process with well-defined boundaries and a result you can act on right away. Here is how it works, how long it takes and what you get at the end.

When you need an audit

  • before launching a project that accepts payments or stores personal data;
  • after a major update or after connecting payments;
  • when changing contractors — to understand what exactly you are taking over;
  • after an incident — to find out how the attacker got into the system;
  • at the request of partners or investors;
  • for a running project — at least once a year.

Black box, gray box and white box

The approach depends on what access the auditor has.

ApproachWhat the auditor hasWhen to choose it
Black boxOnly the website address or the bot's name — like an external attackerTo check what is visible from the outside when there is no access to the code
Gray boxTest accounts with different rolesThe main option: tests access rights and business logic
White boxAccess to the source code and settingsThe most thorough: finds what is invisible from the outside and takes less time

Audit stages

  1. Scope and access. We agree on what we test: domains, bots, APIs, environments. We sign an NDA, agree on the timing and, if possible, get a test copy so as not to disturb your customers. This usually takes one day.
  2. Reconnaissance. We build up the picture: subdomains, open ports, technologies and versions, public files, API endpoints. At this stage we often find forgotten test environments and publicly accessible backups.
  3. Automated scanning. The ZAP and Nuclei scanners look for typical problems, Trivy checks dependencies and containers for known vulnerabilities, Semgrep analyzes the code, and TruffleHog searches the repository for passwords and keys.
  4. Manual testing. The most important part. In Burp Suite, we check whether it is possible to get other people's data, bypass payment, tamper with parameters, escalate privileges or abuse promo codes and bonuses. Scanners do not see such vulnerabilities: formally, the system works “as designed”.
  5. Report. For each finding — a description, steps to reproduce, the risk level and recommendations for fixing it. Plus a short executive summary without technical details.
  6. Review and fixes. We go through the report together with you or your developers. We can fix everything ourselves or answer your team's questions.
  7. Re-check. We make sure that the vulnerabilities are closed and the fixes have not created new ones. With us, it is free.

What is in the report

Each finding gets a level on the CVSS scale — the generally accepted vulnerability scoring system:

LevelCVSS scoreWhat it means
Critical9.0–10.0Fix immediately: a breach or data leak is possible
High7.0–8.9Fix soon
Medium4.0–6.9Schedule a fix
Low0.1–3.9Fix when convenient

This is what entries in the report look like:

RiskFindingWhat to do
HighAccess to other customers' orders by numberCheck on the server that the order belongs to the user
MediumNo limit on admin login attemptsLimit the number of attempts and enable two-factor authentication
LowWeb server version in responsesHide versions in the response headers

We broke down the most common risks in the articles “OWASP Top 10 in plain words” and “7 common Telegram bot vulnerabilities”.

How much it costs and how long it takes

A Telegram bot audit starts from ₽25,000, a website or web service from ₽40,000, a Mini App together with the bot and backend from ₽50,000. The timeline is from 3 days. The cost depends on the amount of code, the number of user roles, integrations and API endpoints.

How to prepare

  • create test accounts for each role: customer, manager, administrator;
  • briefly describe the main scenarios and integrations: payments, CRM, 1C;
  • prepare a test copy without real personal data, if possible;
  • assign a developer who will answer questions;
  • warn your hosting provider or DDoS protection service so that the review is not mistaken for an attack.
