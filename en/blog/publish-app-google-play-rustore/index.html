How to publish an app on Google Play and RuStore

How to publish an Android app on Google Play and RuStore: a developer account, closed testing, the data safety form, build signing, review and updates. How the stores differ, what to prepare in advance and why apps get rejected.

The app is ready — all that is left is to put it in the stores. Projects often lose weeks at this stage: the materials are not ready, the developer account is registered to the wrong company, review sends the build back with comments. Here is how to get through publishing on Google Play and RuStore calmly and what to prepare in advance.

Why publish in two stores

Google Play is the main app store for Android. RuStore is a Russian app store preinstalled on smartphones sold in Russia. Some users look for apps in only one of them, so for a Russian audience we publish in both.

There is also a practical reason: since 2022, Google Play's billing system has been unavailable to users in Russia. An app can accept payments for physical goods and services through YooKassa or SBP, Russia's Faster Payments System, and for digital purchases RuStore has its own payment SDK.

What to prepare in advance

  • Texts: the name, a short and a full description.
  • Graphics: a 512×512 icon, phone screenshots and a feature graphic for the app page.
  • A privacy policy at a public link — without it, the app will not be approved.
  • Forms: about what data the app collects and why, and about the age rating.
  • A test account for reviewers if users need to log in to the app.
  • Support contacts: email and website.

Publishing on Google Play

  1. Developer account. It is better to register it to your company right away: an organization needs a D-U-N-S number, which is free but takes time to get. Then comes identity and contact verification.
  2. App listing. We create the app in Play Console, upload the texts and graphics and fill in the forms.
  3. Signing and build. We enable Play App Signing and upload the build in AAB format.
  4. Closed testing. It is mandatory for new personal accounts: at least 12 testers must use the app for 14 days in a row, and only then can you apply for production access. Organization accounts do not have this requirement, but a test is still useful.
  5. Review and release. We submit the app for review and release it — gradually, if you wish, starting with a share of users.

Publishing on RuStore

  1. Registration in the RuStore Console and verification of the company details.
  2. App listing: texts, graphics, category, age rating.
  3. Build. We upload an APK or AAB and submit it for review.
  4. Store SDKs. We connect RuStore push notifications, payments and reviews — they have versions for Unity, which we use to build apps.

The signing key is one of the most valuable files in the project. In RuStore, the signature of a new version must match the signature of the previous one, so keep the key and its password in a safe place, not just on the developer's computer. In Google Play, Google keeps the app signing key, and a lost upload key can be replaced through support.

Why apps get rejected

  • there is no privacy policy, or it contradicts the data safety form;
  • the app requests unnecessary permissions — for example, access to location without a clear reason;
  • the reviewer could not log in because no test account was provided;
  • the app crashes or does not work without internet, and there is no error message;
  • the description and screenshots do not match what is inside;
  • payment rules are violated.

Review comments are usually specific: we fix them and submit a new build — and the app usually passes on the second attempt.

Updates after release

  • Roll out updates gradually so that a bug is seen by a small share of users rather than by everyone at once.
  • Monitor stability: crash and freeze (ANR) reports reveal problems earlier than reviews do.
  • Plan an annual update. Every year, Google Play raises its target Android version requirement, and apps that are not updated stop being shown to new users on recent devices.
