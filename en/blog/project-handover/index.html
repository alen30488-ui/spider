How to hand over a website or bot to a new team without losing anything

What to get from your previous contractor when changing teams: access, source code, databases, tokens, app signing keys and documentation. A project handover checklist, legal nuances and how a new team takes over someone else's code without downtime.

Contractors get changed for various reasons: the team can no longer keep up, prices have gone up, the project has outgrown the contractor, or the relationship simply has not worked out. The main risk is not the change of people but the loss of access and knowledge about the project. Here is what to take over, in what order to act and how to keep the website or bot running.

Access checklist

What to take overWhy you need it
DomainAccess to the registrar account: without it, you can lose the domain at renewal
DNSThe website, email, subdomains and certificates depend on its records
Hosting or cloudServers, databases and backups
RepositoryAll the code and its change history
Build and stagingHow code gets to the server and where it is tested before release
DatabasesCustomer data, orders, content — and their backups
External servicesPayment systems, CRM, email newsletters, maps, analytics
Telegram botThe account the bot is registered under in @BotFather, and its token
Google Play and RuStoreDeveloper accounts and the app signing key
DocumentationHow to deploy the project, which settings are needed, how the integrations work

First — whose name everything is in

The domain, hosting, developer accounts and payment system accounts must belong to your company. If any of them is registered to the contractor or one of their employees, start the handover by transferring ownership: everything else can be restored, but a domain or an app store account cannot always be recovered.

Keys, passwords and tokens

  • The app signing key. In RuStore, the signature of a new version must match the signature of the previous one, so without the key, releasing updates becomes a problem. Make sure you have the key itself and its password. More on this in the article “How to publish an app on Google Play and RuStore”.
  • The bot token. After the handover, it is worth reissuing it in @BotFather — the old token will stop working immediately.
  • Passwords and API keys. Change the passwords for servers, databases and services and reissue integration keys. The previous team should not keep any access.

Make a fresh backup before changing passwords: if something goes wrong, you will have something to roll back to.

How a new team takes over a project

  1. Audit. We get to grips with the stack, the architecture and the state of the code and check the dependencies for known vulnerabilities.
  2. Deployment to a staging environment. We build the project from what was handed over. If it does not build, something is missing — and it is better to find out while the previous team is still in touch.
  3. A list of risks and a plan. What to fix urgently, what can wait, what is worth redoing.
  4. Documentation. We describe what is missing: how to deploy the project, where the settings are stored, how the integrations work.
  5. First tasks. We start with small changes to test the release process without putting the website at risk.

How to keep things running

  • Do not part ways with the previous team until access has been checked. First, the new team makes sure everything works — then the old team loses access.
  • Agree on a transition period. Two to four weeks of consultations save days of digging through someone else's code.
  • Freeze major changes. While the project is changing hands, release only urgent fixes.
  • Change access at a quiet time, not on a Friday evening or in the middle of a sale.

The legal side

Check your contract with the previous contractor: the exclusive rights to the code and design should pass to you, and the transfer should be confirmed by a signed acceptance certificate. If this is missing, the code may formally belong to the contractor, and it is worth settling this before the relationship ends.

If the previous team is unavailable

It happens: the contractor has disappeared and there is no access. Start with what belongs to you: the domain — through the registrar, the hosting — through its support, using your company documents. The code can often be recovered from the server even if the repository is unavailable. This takes longer and costs more, so it is better to collect access in advance, while everything is fine.

We explained what support after the handover includes and how to choose a contractor in the article “Website support: what is included and how much it costs”.
